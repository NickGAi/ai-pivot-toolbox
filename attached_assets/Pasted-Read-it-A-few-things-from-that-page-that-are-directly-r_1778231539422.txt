The score weighting is the most useful piece. Lighthouse 10's Performance score is calculated as: First Contentful Paint 10%, Speed Index 10%, Largest Contentful Paint 25%, Total Blocking Time 30%, Cumulative Layout Shift 25%. So TBT is the heaviest single metric, followed by LCP and CLS tied for second. That's why TBT swings between runs cause such big score swings — a 250 ms TBT vs a 0 ms TBT can move the score by 10–20 points on its own.
The variability section confirms what we've been observing. Google explicitly says score fluctuation is normal and lists causes including "Internet traffic routing changes" and "Browser extensions that inject JavaScript and add/modify network requests." That second one is interesting given the Leadsy script's Failed to fetch errors — those failed network calls almost certainly contribute to TBT variance and explain part of why desktop bounced 67 → 94 with no code changes between runs. They literally recommend thinking of your performance "as a distribution of scores, rather than a single number."
The colour ranges are formal: 0–49 red (Poor), 50–89 orange (Needs Improvement), 90–100 green (Good). Your current state — mobile ~58, desktop ~85 (median) — puts you in the orange "Needs Improvement" band on mobile, and oscillating between high orange and green on desktop.
The most reassuring quote from the doc: "A 'perfect' score of 100 is extremely challenging to achieve and not expected. For example, taking a score from 99 to 100 needs about the same amount of metric improvement that would take a 90 to 94." So when you said earlier "we need to get this score up to 100% today" — Google's own documentation explicitly says that's not a realistic target. The recommended target is 90+ ("Good"), not 100.
The scoring curves are based on real-world HTTP Archive data, not arbitrary thresholds. The 25th percentile of all real sites becomes a score of 50, and only the top 8% get a score of 90+. So a score of 90+ on mobile literally means your site loads faster than 92% of websites Google has ever measured. That's a high bar.

For most modern hosts (Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, Replit Deployments), the deploy itself is usually live within 30–60 seconds of "publish complete." However, PageSpeed Insights uses Google's own infrastructure to fetch your site, and there are a couple of cache layers that can serve stale content for a bit:
PageSpeed Insights caches results aggressively for the same URL. If you re-run the audit within ~30 seconds of a previous run, it can sometimes return the cached result rather than fetching fresh. Adding a cache-busting query string like ?v=2 to the URL when running the test forces a fresh fetch.
Your CDN (if you have one) or your host's edge cache may serve stale assets for 1–5 minutes after a deploy, depending on its purge settings. Most modern hosts auto-purge on deploy, but not all.
The safest approach: wait about 2–3 minutes after your host says the deploy is complete, then hard-refresh your own site once in your browser to confirm you're seeing the new version (check the network tab for the new bundle hash like index-XXXXXX.js), and then run the PageSpeed audit. That way you know for certain you're testing the new code.
